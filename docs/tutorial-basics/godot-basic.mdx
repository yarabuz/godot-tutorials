# Godot Basics

## 4 key Concepts
<hr/>

![Godot Key Concepts](img/basic/godot-key-concepts.jpg)

There are 4 key concepts to master in godot:

- Nodes
- Scenes
- Scene Tree
- Signals

### Nodes
<hr/>

![Godot Nodes](img/basic/1-nodes.jpg)

A scene is composed of one or more *nodes*.<br/>
*Nodes* are your game's smallest building blocks that you arrange into trees. Here's a list of nodes.<br/>

![Godot List of Nodes](img/basic/nodes.jpg)

### Scene
<hr/>

![Godot Scene](img/basic/2-scenes.jpg)

In Godot, you break down your game in reusable *scenes*.<br/>
A *scene* can be a character, a weapon, a menu in the user interface, a single house, an entire level, or anything you can think of.<br/>
Godot's scenes are flexible; they fill the role of both prefabs and scenes in some other game engines.<br/>
 <br/>

![Godot Scenes](img/basic/scenes.jpg)

### Scene Tree
<hr/>

![Godot Scene Tree](img/basic/3-scene-tree.jpg)

All your game's scenes come together in the *scene tree*, literally a tree of scenes.<br/>
And as scenes are trees of nodes, the *scene tree* also is a tree of nodes.<br/>
But it's easier to think of your game in terms of scenes as they can represent characters, weapons, doors, or your user interface.<br/>
 <br/>

![Godot Scene Tree Example](img/basic/root-tree.jpg)

### Signals
<hr/>

![Godot Signals](img/basic/4-signals.jpg)

All your game's scenes come together in the *scene tree*, literally a tree of scenes.
Nodes emit signals when some event occurs.<br/>
This feature allows you to make nodes communicate without hard-wiring them in code.<br/>
It gives you a lot of flexibility in how you structure your scenes.<br/>
 <br/>

![Godot Signal Example](img/basic/signals.jpg)
